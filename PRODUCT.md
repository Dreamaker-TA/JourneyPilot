# Product

## Register

product

## Users

JourneyPilot serves individual travellers running a self-hosted, single-user workspace. They arrive with dates, destinations, preferences, and constraints, then need to inspect, adjust, verify, and export a plan without translating internal agent or provider mechanics.

## Product Purpose

Turn an expressed travel intent into one coherent, source-backed itinerary workspace. Success means the itinerary, map, weather, sources, report, and PDF agree; hard requirements are never silently violated; uncertain claims stay visibly uncertain; and long-running work remains controllable and recoverable.

## Brand Personality

Calm, trustworthy, and precise. The interface should feel like a well-kept travel drafting desk: considered and warm enough for planning, but operationally clear when the user needs to make a decision.

## Anti-references

Do not resemble an opaque chatbot that buries the deliverable in prose, a diagnostic console that exposes candidate IDs or prompt internals, or a decorative travel moodboard that trades legibility and evidence for atmosphere. Avoid dense warning noise, gratuitous animation, and dark technical surfaces on the primary consumer workspace.

## Design Principles

- Keep the itinerary as the primary working truth; every other surface is its consistent projection.
- Put evidence and uncertainty next to the claim they qualify, with deeper diagnostics behind intentional inspection.
- Make the user's requirements and any honest deviations understandable without exposing internal scoring or orchestration.
- Preserve control over long-running work with explicit confirmation, recovery, cancellation, and reversible edits.
- Prefer familiar, restrained product interactions over decorative or agent-centric UI.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA contrast and keyboard access across the product. Maintain visible focus, at least 44 px touch targets where interaction density permits, reduced-motion behavior, semantic status labels that do not rely on color alone, resilient CJK wrapping, and clear treatment of accessibility requirements in the travel contract.
